8.2 Oil cycle
Oil runs all the way along the refrigeration circuit together with the refrigerant. in the refrigerant. In the
compressor a very small amount (approx. 5%) of hot oil
passes the bearings and enters the chamber to be
compressed and discharged together with the refrigerant into the circuit. The oil flows with the hot superheated gas within the discharge line in the form of minute oil drops (as in fog) along to the condenser.
After the refrigerant phase change in the condenser,
the oil becomes completely invisible within the liquid refrigerant and is transported up to the evaporator passing through the sight glass, filter and automatic expansion valve. After the refrigerant has completely evaporated the oil is transported away in the form of a thin film
on the inside tube wall of the suction line by the gas
stream of the refrigerant.
Refrigerant leakages thus also cause oil traces to be
left at the location of the leakage. Any oil traces found
along the refrigerant system should be investigated for
possible refrigerant leakages.
