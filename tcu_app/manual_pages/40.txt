8.3 Basic physical aspects
Every refrigeration circuit is divided into a high pressure and a low pressure side. The compressor (discharge side), discharge line, condenser, sight glass, filter and automatic expansion valve together make up
the high pressure side. The pressure is nearly the
same on all mentioned positions (no pressure loss).
This in turns means that the discharge pressure, condensation pressure and saturation pressure are all
equal at 7.7 bar abs. Whilst the pressure is the same
for the above mentioned components, the refrigerant
temperature is however different for all of the positions
that make up the high pressure side. The superheated
refrigerant gas has a temperature of about 70°C on
leaving the compressor. By the time the refrigerant liquid leaves the condenser, it has a temperature of only
30°C (condensation temperature). This temperature is
maintained until the refrigerant reaches the automatic
expansion valve. The low pressure side of the refrigeration circuit commences after the automatic expansion
device with the evaporator, then the suction line and
last the compressor (suction side). As with the high
pressure side of the circuit the pressure on all components of the low pressure side is roughly the same (no
pressure loss) and therefore evaporation pressure,
saturation pressure and suction pressure are all equal
to 3,3 bar abs. Similarly to the high pressure side, the
temperature on the low pressure side varies as the refrigerant travels through the various components from
the evaporator back to the compressor. The refrigerant
boils at 3°C in the evaporator (saturation pressure 3,3
bar abs) and superheats up to nearly temperature controlled water temperature. This is also the temperature
of the refrigerant in the suction line and the compressor
entrance temperature.
